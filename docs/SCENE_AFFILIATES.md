# The scene: Japanese culture + gaming — merchant shortlist

Working list for Brayton (Dean). Mined from the GoAffPro master list on
24 Aug 2026, then re-cut when the brief changed from "a gaming site" to
something better.

## The reframe, and why it is the right one

> "Gaming is not just gonna be gaming... it can tie into Japanese culture in
> general, and maybe kinda be a link over to Kawaii Katz."
>
> "In general, gaming culture too. Not just Japanese... This needs to be the
> coolest fucking GameStop meets Disc Replay meets every arcade you've ever
> loved."

**GameStop x Disc Replay x arcade is the sharper of the two, and it settles the
site's shape rather than just its subject.** Those are BROWSE-first shops. Nobody
walks into Disc Replay with a part number; they walk the crates. So this site is
a room to wander, not a search box with a price table — which is the opposite of
Legal-Leaf Market's own design, where somebody arrives knowing they want an
ounce and wants the cheapest one.

That also promotes the arcade from a gimmick to the thesis. An arcade in a shop
that sells arcade cabinets is not a novelty feature; it is the shop.

That is a stronger position than a gaming affiliate site, for three reasons:

1. **A scene has a reason to be visited twice.** "Cheapest gaming mouse" is a
   search you run once. A scene is somewhere people come back to.
2. **It already has a sibling.** Kawaii Katz aggregates kawaii products from
   eight Shopify vendors — it IS a Japanese-culture affiliate site, just scoped
   narrowly. This becomes its sibling rather than an orphan, and the two
   cross-link instead of competing.
3. **It survives the cannabis-adjacency problem** that keeps this off
   Legal-Leaf Market's own domain, and it gives Brayton something that is his.

**The numbers below are the GoAffPro half only.** 22,290 merchants in the file.
Mined across the whole scene — retro and arcade, peripherals, tabletop and TCG,
anime and figures, kawaii and decora, pop culture and media, snacks — **98
survive filtering and 16 have a cookie worth having.**

And the shape of what survives is itself the finding:

| Category | Merchants in 22,290 |
|---|---|

| Peripherals & battlestation | 17 |
| Pop culture & media | 15 |

**The two categories the concept is named after are the two GoAffPro barely
has.** Retro and arcade is 7 merchants; of those only GoRetrogame (10%/45-day)
has a window worth having. So the Disc Replay half of this site cannot be built
out of GoAffPro at all — it needs direct outreach, and the used/collector market
is largely eBay, Mercari and independent shops rather than affiliate networks.
That is worth knowing before anybody promises a retro catalogue.

Everything below is a GoAffPro merchant, which means: Shopify store, self-serve
signup, approval usually in days rather than weeks. That is the whole reason to
start here.

## Read this before applying to anything

**GoAffPro is a Shopify app, so this list is 22,000 small independent Shopify
stores.** There is no Razer, no Secretlab, no Humble Bundle in it — those run on
Impact, CJ and Awin. GoAffPro will not carry a "biggest gaming affiliate site";
what it will do is get real merchants live fast and cover a long tail the big
affiliate sites ignore. That is a differentiator, not a consolation prize.

**82 of the 111 have a 7-day cookie or shorter.** For an impulse buy that is
survivable. For a $200 chair or a mechanical keyboard — the purchases people
actually research — a 7-day window means most of the money leaks away. Both
tables below are sorted by COOKIE first for that reason, not by headline rate.

**A high rate is usually a warning.** The four highest-commission gaming rows in
this entire file are all things not to touch. They are listed at the bottom.

## The anime cluster — read this before applying to twelve of them

Twenty domains in the file follow one pattern: `animekeycaps.com`,
`anime-mousepad.com`, `animestatue.com`, `animeswimsuit.com`, `animebed.com`,
`animebackpack.com`, `animekimono.com`, `animepuzzle.com`, `animejacket.com`,
`otakutreat.shop`, `otaku4goods.com`, `otaku-palace-shop`... nearly all at 15
percent on a 7-day cookie, all named the same way.

**That is one operator running a dropship network, not twenty merchants.** The
catalogues are near-certainly the same stock. Applying to a dozen of them is
applying to one company, and listing all twelve on a comparison site would mean
publishing the same products twelve times under different names — which is
exactly what makes a comparison site worthless.

Pick **two at most** — keycaps and mousepads have the genuine search demand —
and treat the rest as one merchant you already have.

## What to do after this list

1. **Apply to Tier 1 and the three good kawaii merchants.** Self-serve, days
   rather than weeks. Ten live merchants is enough to launch a real catalogue
   and enough to make the direct-outreach applications credible.
2. **Build the site as a fork of Legal-Leaf Market, not from scratch.**
   `api/products.js` already reads Shopify `/products.json`, and nearly every
   merchant here is Shopify — several are literally `*.myshopify.com`. Swap the
   `STORES` list and keep the engine, the rails, the cards, the `?shelf=`
   slicing and the arcade. Days, not months.
3. **Decide the Kawaii Katz relationship before building, not after.** It is
   already a Japanese-culture affiliate site over eight Shopify vendors. Two
   sensible shapes: fold the scene into it, or run a sibling and cross-link
   hard. I would run the sibling — "Kawaii Katz" does not say gaming, and a
   sharp brand beats a broad one — but with **one merchant registry serving
   both**, sliced per site the way `api/shelves.js` slices this one. One
   catalogue, two storefronts. The friction is that Kawaii Katz is Next.js and
   this engine is not, so that decision costs something either way.
4. **Then apply to the real networks from that domain** — Razer (3-10%),
   Secretlab (up to 12%, 45-day cookie), SteelSeries, Humble Bundle (10%). Those
   want a site with pages on it, and they want a domain with no cannabis
   adjacency. That last point is the entire reason this is Brayton's site rather
   than a section of Legal-Leaf Market.
