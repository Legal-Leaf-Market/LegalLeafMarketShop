## Fact Sheet — What's Actually Happening

**The critical framing:** the ban has **not** been delayed. The Senate voted to delay it. That's different, and it's the whole story.

**The baseline.** The 2018 Farm Bill defined hemp as under 0.3% delta-9 THC by dry weight. That definition built the hemp-derived THC market — reported at roughly **$28–28.8 billion**.

**The ban.** A spending bill signed in late 2025 redefined hemp so that only products with **0.4 milligrams of total THC per container** stay legal. Per container, not per serving. Effective date: **November 12, 2026.** That is still operative law today.

**The push-back.** Early Saturday, **August 8, 2026**, the Senate passed a continuing resolution **90–6** funding the government through December 11 — and it moves the hemp ban's effective date to **December 11, 2026.**

**The amendment fight.** Sen. Ted Budd (R-NC) filed **S.Amdt. 6733** to strip the delay and hold November 12. Sen. Amy Klobuchar (D-MN) moved to table it. **The motion to table carried 61–32** — so Budd's amendment was killed procedurally, not voted down on the merits. Budd's cosponsors included McConnell, Cornyn, Grassley, and Gillibrand.

**Why nothing is safe yet.** The CR is **Senate-passed only.** The **House has not acted and is in recess until August 31, 2026.** If the House doesn't pass it, **November 12 remains the law.** That's roughly ten weeks on the calendar from the House's return — and far fewer actual legislative days.

**A carve-out survives either way.** Synthetic cannabinoids "not capable of being naturally produced by a Cannabis sativa L. plant" are still banned **November 12, 2026**, delay or no delay. Related: the DEA filed a brief in mid-August arguing HHC is a Schedule I controlled substance rather than lawful hemp.

**The commercial clock is already running.** Square told sellers a new federal law takes effect November 12 and that they must **remove CBD and hemp products from their catalogs by October 15, 2026.** Curaleaf announced it would exit the hemp THC sector back in December 2025. Processors and payment platforms are pricing in the ban regardless of what Congress does.

**It's not breaking along party lines.** Thune (R-SD) and Schumer (D-NY) both voted to table Budd's amendment. Sen. Rand Paul (R-KY) said the cannabis industry and "**some of** the alcohol industry" dislike the delay because it competes — meanwhile alcohol retailers, wholesalers, and the Beverage Alcohol Merchants Coalition publicly *support* regulation over prohibition. Budd said the President "has been misinformed by his staff." CMS **Administrator** Dr. Mehmet Oz wrote to senators asking them to oppose S.Amdt. 6733, citing a plan to cover up to **$500/year** of hemp products through three CMS innovation models (ACO REACH, Enhancing Oncology, LEAD). On Aug 17, Paul posted that "Washington is three months from wiping out the hemp industry nationwide."

**Two bills would replace prohibition with regulation:**

| Bill | Sponsors | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Beverage Regulatory Parity Act**<br>*introduced Aug 10, 2026* | Reps. Beth Van Duyne (R-TX), Greg Landsman (D-OH) | 21+ only · **5 mg** total intoxicating THC per serving · **8¢/mg** federal tax · TTB + HHS + USDA oversight · U.S.-cultivated naturally occurring cannabinoids only · 750 mL cap on multi-serve containers · federal permits for manufacturers, wholesalers, sellers · states, tribes, and localities may be more stringent but can't block interstate transport |
| **Lawful Hemp Protection Act**<br>**H.R. 9830** · *introduced July 22, 2026* | Reps. Andy Barr (R-KY), Angie Craig (D-MN); co-led by Tim Moore (R-NC), Marc Veasey (D-TX) | National regulatory framework for hemp-derived products |

**State-level pressure, same week.** A federal judge declined to delay Virginia's THC/hemp rule change (Aug 14). Hawaii officials filed their defense against an industry suit over that state's hemp crackdown (Aug 16).

**The ask.** The U.S. Hemp Roundtable runs constituent contact campaigns at **hempsupporter.com** — urging House members to cosponsor the Lawful Hemp Protection Act and to keep hemp protections in appropriations.

## Before You Post

**1. Re-verify the operative date the morning you post.** The House returns Aug 31. If it passes the CR, your framing flips from "Nov 12 is still law" to "Dec 11 is the new date" — and Posts 1 and 5 need a rewrite, not a tweak. The weekly monitoring task I set up will flag this.

**2. Instagram's actual policy** (Meta Community Standards, Restricted Goods & Services): advocacy, discussion, and education are **permitted**. What's prohibited is transactional — "content that attempts to buy, sell, trade, donate or gift or asks for marijuana and products containing THC," with parallel language covering ingestible and non-ingestible CBD. Business content from dispensaries is allowed behind an 18+ age gate with no attempt to transact. So:
- These posts are policy-compliant as written
- No product photography, prices, menus, or "shop now" on any of them
- Point bio to hempsupporter.com or a policy page while these run — not a storefront
- Keep THC references legislative, never experiential
- No health or therapeutic claims, full stop
- Enforcement has historically over-blocked hemp accounts even within policy, so expect some reach suppression and don't read it as a violation

**3. Numbers you can state with confidence:** CR passed 90–6. Motion to table S.Amdt. 6733 carried 61–32. 0.4 mg/container. 5 mg/serving cap in the parity bill. 8¢/mg tax. 750 mL container cap. H.R. 9830. Oct 15 Square deadline. Nov 12 and Dec 11 dates. All verified.

**4. One thing to avoid stating:** the Beverage Regulatory Parity Act's **H.R. number** — it wasn't published in any available source as of Aug 17. Name the bill and sponsors, not a number. (H.R. 9830 for the Lawful Hemp Protection Act *is* confirmed.)

**5. Two hedges to keep:** the **$28 billion** figure is trade-press sizing, not a government estimate — "reported at roughly" is the honest framing. And Rand Paul said "**some of** the alcohol industry," not the whole industry; that distinction matters because alcohol retailers and wholesalers are on your side of this.
