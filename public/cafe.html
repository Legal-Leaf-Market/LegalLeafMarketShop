coffee, tea & the corner shopLegal Leaf Cafe

A neighborhood room for the people who already know the plants. espresso · loose-leaf tea · herbs · a bodega shelf · live acoustic music

Legal-Leaf Market started as a screen: a place to compare what is out there. The cafe is the other half, a real counter, a real kettle and a stage the size of a rug. It opens in phases, and only when each phase is cleared with the people who have to clear it.

I

why we are doing this

A shop with no front door

Everything we have built so far lives on a phone. It is useful, and it is also about as far from a neighborhood as a business can get. Nobody has ever run into a friend at a comparison table.

The idea behind the cafe is old and simple: a room where you can get a good cup of coffee or a pot of loose-leaf tea, pick up a few things off a small shelf, sit for an hour and hear somebody play. Part cafe, part bodega, part living room. We want it to feel handmade and a little bohemian, and we want it to be the sort of place a regular can walk into without being sold anything.

It is also deliberately small. We would rather open a tidy room we can run properly than a big one we cannot, so the first phase is built to need as little infrastructure as possible while the harder questions get answered.

IV

the sound of the place

A small stage with a rug under it

Live acoustic music is part of the idea from the start, not an afterthought. We are picturing a low riser in the corner, an area rug, a stool and a quiet room: solo players and duos, early evenings, the kind of set you can talk over if you want to and listen to if you do not. No sound system bigger than it needs to be.

It waits for phase 1B, because a stage needs answers we do not have yet: what the space allows for noise and occupancy, what music licensing the venue needs for live or recorded music, and how it all fits around the health department’s rules for the room.

Play, or know someone who does? We would like to hear from local musicians long before the doors open. Write to hello@legalleafmarket.shop. There is no booking process yet, only a list of people we want to call first.

V

how it opens

Two phases, in this order

Food-service rules decide a lot about what a cafe can do on day one. Indiana replaced its old retail food code with a new one, 410 IAC 7-26, which took effect on April 16, 2025. We are planning against the new code, and any reference to the old one is out of date.

Phase 1A

Open the simple version

  • Single-use cups and tea filter bags, so customers are not handed reusable dishes
  • A small, well-run brew bar
  • The herb and tea shelf
  • A handwashing sink, and whatever dish setup the health department requires for the bar tools we reuse
Phase 1B

Add the rest, once cleared

  • The mug wall and refill program
  • Glass and gear, after the tobacco sales certificate
  • The bodega shelf
  • Live music nights

What still has to be answered, in writing

  • Whether the local health department accepts the Phase 1A setup, including what a compact three-compartment dish arrangement would need to look like for the bar tools
  • Whether the refill process for customer-owned mugs meets 410 IAC 7-26
  • Plan review, permit, zoning, fire and occupancy requirements for the actual space
  • Insurance, since policies often exclude hemp products and smoking accessories
  • Music licensing for live performance

VI

the rules, and how they are shifting

Hemp law is moving under everyone’s feet

Our shelf is built mostly around plain herbs, coffee and tea, partly because the law around hemp is changing fast. Here is where it stands, in order. as of November 5, 2026

  • 2019 Indiana bans smokable hemp

    Indiana allowed hemp farming after the 2018 federal farm bill, but made smokable hemp illegal. Retailers sued over the ban, and attempts to reverse it in the legislature have not succeeded. It remains in place today.

  • November 2025 Congress redefines “hemp”

    A funding bill that ended the federal shutdown rewrote the legal definition of hemp. Under it, hemp products are capped at 0.4 milligrams of total THC per container, which takes most intoxicating hemp products out of the definition. It was written with a one-year runway.

  • 2026 · Indiana session The state ban on intoxicating hemp does not pass

    Senate Bill 250 would have lined Indiana up with the federal change and put hemp retail under the Alcohol and Tobacco Commission. It cleared the Senate but the session ended without a ban, so Indiana still has no state-level hemp-specific rules for intoxicating products beyond the smokable ban.

  • August – September 2026 Congress buys a month

    A stopgap funding bill moves most of the federal restrictions from November 12 to December 11, 2026. The Senate rejected an attempt to strip the delay by a wide margin. Lawyers covering the industry are blunt that this is a pause, not a safe harbor.

  • November 12, 2026 Synthetic cannabinoids are prohibited

    The delay has a carve-out: the ban on cannabinoids that a hemp plant cannot make naturally still starts on the original date.

  • December 11, 2026 The new total-THC standard takes effect

    Unless Congress acts again, the 0.4 milligram per container limit applies. Many full-spectrum hemp products, and most THCa flower, would fall outside the federal definition of hemp.

  • After December What happens next is open

    The delay was meant to give Congress time to write a regulatory framework for hemp-derived products, and the industry is lobbying for one. Indiana lawmakers could take the question up again in 2027. Federal marijuana scheduling is also in play and could complicate hemp rules further. We have not found a settled proposal for any of these, so we are not guessing at one. This page will be updated as that changes.

What it means for the cafe

The shelf does not depend on any of this: no smokable hemp, no synthetics, and no hemp-derived product at all until its lab report shows the current total THC per container. If the rules shift again, the plan can follow without rebuilding the room.

VII

questions people ask

The short answers

Is it open?

No. There is no opening date yet, and the roadmap above is why. This page is the plan, not a menu.

Where is it?

South Central Indiana. We have not announced an address because we have not signed for one.

Will you sell smokable hemp, delta-8 or marijuana?

No to all three. Indiana bans smokable hemp, we are not stocking synthetic cannabinoids, and Indiana does not have a legal adult-use cannabis market. Any hemp-derived product, such as a CBD tincture, only goes on the shelf if its current lab report fits the total-THC limit that takes effect December 11, 2026.

Can I buy things online in the meantime?

The online shop is separate and lives at shop.legalleafmarket.shop. Nothing on this page can be ordered.

Will there be food?

Not at first. The plan is drinks and packaged goods, and anything more waits on what the health department says.

Is it 21 and over?

Not decided. This website is for adults 21 and over. How the room itself works will depend on what we end up selling and what each license requires, and we will say so plainly before opening.

How do I follow along?

There is no mailing list yet. Write to hello@legalleafmarket.shop and we will reply, and this page will be updated as the checklist moves.
