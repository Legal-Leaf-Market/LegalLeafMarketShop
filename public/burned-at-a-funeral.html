History & archaeology

they burned itat a funeral

Smoke was never the by-product. Smoke was the point.

The oldest chemically verified evidence for anyone smoking cannabis anywhere is about 2,500 years old. It was not found at a party, a market or a farm. It was found in ten wooden bowls, in a cemetery, halfway up a mountain, and the people who left it there were burning it for the dead.

I

c. 500 BCE · the Pamir Plateau

Ten braziers and a chemical signature

In June 2019 a team of archaeologists and chemists published in Science Advances. They had analysed the residue inside ten wooden braziers recovered from burials at the Jirzankal Cemetery, high on the Pamir Plateau in what is now far-western China. Inside each one: small stones that had been heated to a considerable temperature.

The residue came back carrying cannabinol, the compound left behind when THC is burned. Not hemp fibre. Not seed. Combusted, psychoactive cannabis, and at levels that suggested these were not ordinary wild plants but ones chosen or cultivated for potency.

The stones were hot. The residue was cannabinol. The room was a grave.

And that last part is the finding people skip past. This was a mortuary context. The braziers were not left over from an evening in. They were part of what happened when somebody was buried, placed in the tomb, with the body, as equipment for the occasion.

II

and, independently, Herodotus

Somebody wrote it down at almost exactly the same time

What makes the Jirzankal result land harder is that it does not stand alone. Around the same century, Herodotus described the Scythians, steppe peoples whose range touched the same wide world, doing something extremely specific: getting inside a closed tent, throwing hemp onto red-hot stones, and reacting to the vapour that came off it.

Classicists have argued for a very long time about exactly what he was describing, a purification rite, a steam bath, an intoxication, or all three at once, and the text is genuinely ambiguous. But the mechanism he describes is not ambiguous at all. Plant. Hot stones. Enclosed space. Breathe.

Which is precisely the apparatus that turned up in the ground two and a half thousand years later, with the chemistry still on it.

III

the pattern underneath

Smoke was how you talked to whatever was above you

Step back far enough and Jirzankal stops looking exotic and starts looking typical. For almost all of recorded human history, smoke was not a side effect of worship. It was the medium of it.

Burnt offerings and temple incense in the Hebrew Bible. The censer swung down the aisle of a cathedral. Incense across Buddhist, Hindu, Shinto and Taoist practice. Psalm 141 asks, in as many words, for a prayer to rise like incense.

The logic is visual and it needs no theology whatsoever to follow. You cannot see a prayer. You can see smoke. It leaves your hands and it goes up, and it keeps going after you have stopped watching. Across traditions with no contact between them, people looked at that and drew the same conclusion.

Smoke was the visible part of the prayer. Most cultures did this at a funeral long before they did it for fun.

IV

while we are counting

And the material is older than the ritual by a distance

Since we are already dealing in thousands of years, it is worth noting they are the short end of this. THC is C21H30O2. The hydrogen in it was forged in the first minutes after the Big Bang and essentially none has been made since. The carbon and the oxygen could not exist yet, nothing in the early universe could build them. They had to be fused inside stars, and those stars had to die and scatter the results, before there was an Earth for any of it to land on.

  1. 13.8 billion years agoHydrogen. The universe makes almost nothing else.
  2. inside the first starsCarbon and oxygen are manufactured, then scattered by stars dying.
  3. 4.5 billion years agoEarth condenses out of that debris, carrying the atoms with it.
  4. ~470 million years agoPlants move onto land and begin an arms race with everything that wants to eat them.
  5. c. 500 BCETen braziers, a mountain cemetery, and a chemical signature that survives to be read.
  6. nowYou are comparing it by the gram, on a phone.

What this is not

This is not a health claim. Nothing above says cannabis is safe, beneficial, or medicinal. It says people burned it a long time ago, which is a fact about history and not about your lungs. We make no comparative safety claims about any product on this site.

Old is not the same as good. Plenty of things are ancient and terrible. The antiquity of a practice is an interesting fact, not an argument for doing it, and anybody using "humans have always done this" as a health case is selling you something.

This is not an invitation to treat anybody's ceremony as decor. Living sacred and ceremonial plant traditions exist in many cultures right now. They are not historical curiosities and they are not marketing material. We are pointing at a broad human pattern that includes incense and resin in a dozen traditions. We are not claiming a piece of anyone's rite.
